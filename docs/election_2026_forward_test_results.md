## Research question and frozen hypothesis

Do contracts whose **best ask** is in [0.20, 0.30) seven days before their scheduled end settle YES more often than that ask implies? The rules were fixed on 2026-10-21, before any data was collected, and have not changed:

- Universe: open binary markets with an order book and at least **$100,000 traded at the moment of entry** (not final volume).
- Entry: the first collector run at which the **scheduled end** is 7 days or less away (within a day of the 7-day mark).
- Position: **one contract of the first-listed outcome** (its name is shown in the table below), bought at the **best ask** in [0.20, 0.30), held until the market officially settles.
- Settlement: the exchange's final result, matched by the exact outcome token. Race calls, news and current prices never settle a position.

## Why the results may differ from the historical study

- **Sample size:** 17 settled positions against 390. A single win more or fewer moves the win rate by 5.9 points.
- **Related outcomes:** 10 are election markets, entered in 1 run(s) (2026-10-28). Races decided on the same election day are not independent of each other, even across different events (e.g. a national swing affects every governor race).
- **Executable prices:** the forward test pays the best ask; the historical study used recorded prices, which sit closer to the midpoint.
- **Selection:** the 20–30 cent range was chosen after looking at the historical data, so part of its historical edge may be selection luck.

## Limitations

- Fewer than 100 settled positions: no verdict, wide intervals, and the bootstrap can't fully reflect dependence between different election events.
- 3 positions can't be re-checked against raw books (artifacts expired); their quotes rest on the stored entry only.
- Entry times are the run's start; the book was read a few seconds later.
- No fees, no market impact of our own orders, no partial fills: a filled paper order isn't a real fill.
- 3 positions are unresolved; the result can change when they settle.

## Conclusion: evidence versus speculation

- **Evidence:** 17 real-quote Group A positions have settled; 2 won. At the best ask this lost $2.16 on $4.16 (-51.9%). The quotes behind 17 positions are verified against raw order books.
- **Not evidence (yet):** whether Group A has an edge. The forward test has added new, honestly recorded data points that so far point the other way from the historical study, but they are too few to support or reject it.
- **Speculation (not tested):** that election favourites were simply priced well in 2026, or that the historical edge came from recorded prices that couldn't be traded.

## What the next prospective experiment should test

- Keep the frozen test running unchanged until 100 positions settle; report them by month and with election markets separately.
- Deploy the upgraded collector (request/response/exchange timestamps, full books stored in the database, invalid books flagged) and verify a cloud run before relying on it.
- For events like elections, fix in advance how correlated contracts are counted (e.g. one position per event-day cluster), before the data comes in.
- Write the election pilot's own analysis plan before looking at its settlements.
